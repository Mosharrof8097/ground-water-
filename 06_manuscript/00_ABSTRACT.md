Monitoring trace metals in groundwater requires laboratory instrumentation that
is scarcest where contamination is most widespread. Whether parameters a field
team can measure, such as pH, redox potential, conductance, iron and manganese,
can stand in for that analysis has not been tested under validation accounting
for spatial structure, left censoring and transfer. Using 271 wells of the BGS
and DPHE National Hydrochemical Survey of Bangladesh, with external validation
on 1,027 wells in fifteen unseen districts, we screened thirteen trace metals
across five predictor tiers ordered by cost. Six metals proved unmodellable, in
five cases for analytical rather than geochemical reasons: chromium, cadmium
and copper were reported as detection limits in 97 %, 84 % and 81 % of wells.
What governs this is the detection limit relative to the distribution, not the
censoring rate. Uranium tolerates 23 % censoring without consequence, whereas
arsenic, censored in 34 % at a limit sitting at 27 % of its median, moves from
R² = +0.104 to −0.032 on the substitution rule alone. Spatial blocking cut
feasible target and tier combinations from 23 to 9. Nickel, which random folds
rated at R² = 0.288, fell to −0.204 and failed at every tier. On unseen
districts concentration did not transfer but ranking did: the arsenic model
built on iron, manganese and well depth returned R² = −5.58 yet an area under
the curve of 0.828 against the WHO guideline, and against the Bangladesh
standard identified 63 of 64 contaminated wells while referring 55 % for
laboratory confirmation. Split conformal intervals held 87.2 % to 89.4 %
coverage within the study area but ranged from 68.9 % to 98.8 % on transfer.
Surrogate screening therefore orders wells usefully but cannot quantify them,
and among these metals only arsenic and uranium are both hazardous here and
screenable.

**Keywords:** groundwater; trace metals; left-censored data; spatial cross
validation; conformal prediction; Bangladesh
